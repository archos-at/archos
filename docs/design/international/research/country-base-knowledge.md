# R3: Country base knowledge for architects, and the public touch points that supply it

Research input for the per-country knowledge model (Notion ticket 30), checked against its two main consumers: project setup (ticket 31) and office base knowledge (ticket 32). External research as of 2026-11-04. Depth: Austria, Germany, Switzerland. Samples: France, England, Netherlands, California. Liechtenstein and South Tyrol get short notes.

Confidence markers: claims with a link were checked against that source this session. **[bk]** marks background knowledge I did not re-check this session; verify it before it goes into product data. **[conflict]** marks places where sources disagree.

---

## 1. Findings that shape the model

1. **Most of a country's base knowledge is not text.** Of the roughly 25 knowledge areas below, about a third are mainly corpus (statutes, guidelines, norms, commentary, directives). The rest are structured reference data (building-class thresholds, service-phase tables, fee tables, wind and snow zones by municipality, holiday calendars, deadline rules, municipality codes, cost indices) or live public services (cadastre, zoning, hazards, heritage, addresses, building registers). A model that treats a country as "a corpus plus some metadata" fails the founder's goal and ticket 31.

2. **One decisive fact usually has several providers, and which one wins differs by country.** Snow load in Austria comes from a point lookup ([HORA](https://www.bmluk.gv.at/themen/wasser/schutz-vor-hochwasser/hora-update-2024.html), zones per ÖNORM B 1991-1-3). In Germany it is a table keyed by administrative unit ([DIBt "Zuordnung der Schneelastzonen nach Verwaltungsgrenzen"](https://www.regierung-mv.de/static/Regierungsportal/Ministerium%20f%C3%BCr%20Inneres%20und%20Europa/Inhalte/Bau/Planen%20und%20Bauen/Technische%20Baubestimmungen/Dateien/VVTB_M-V_Stand_05_01_2023%20nebst%20Anlage%20zur%20Ziffer.pdf)). In Switzerland it is a formula from SIA 261 over altitude and zone [bk]. Zoning is a geometry from a GIS service, a code list, and a legal text (the Verordnung or the textliche Festsetzungen), all at once. So the primitive is a **typed fact plus resolvers with precedence and provenance**, not "a zoning connector".

3. **Competent authority is a rule, not a list.** In Austria the mayor is the building authority by default, with exceptions (Vienna's Magistrat, statutory cities, delegations to district authorities) [bk]. In Germany it is the lower building supervisory authority at the Landkreis or kreisfreie Stadt level, sometimes the Große Kreisstadt, and the rule differs by Land [bk]. In Switzerland it is normally the Gemeinde, but projects outside the building zone always need cantonal approval (Art. 25 Abs. 2 RPG) [bk], and some cantons decide centrally ([Basel-Landschaft, Valais](https://www.vs.ch/de/web/sajmte/bauverfahren); [Aargau](https://www.ag.ch/de/themen/planen-bauen/baubewilligungen/zustaendigkeiten)). In Switzerland the authority therefore depends on a zoning fact. Germany alone has a federated, machine-readable competence directory ([XZuFi/PVOG](https://produktportal.pvog.fitko.de/pdfs/BeD_Anbindungsanleitung.pdf)).

4. **Point-in-time law is possible for Austria, Switzerland, France, England and the Netherlands, and patchy for Germany.** Austria's RIS returns a law as it stood on a date through `Fassung.FassungVom`. Historical consolidated state law covers Carinthia and Tyrol from 2011, Styria from 2014, Vienna from mid-2014, Lower Austria from 2015 and Vorarlberg from 2016 ([RIS info](https://ris.bka.gv.at/UI/Info.aspx); [client docs](https://pypi.org/project/at-ris-mcp/)). Switzerland's Fedlex is linked data under ELI with a SPARQL endpoint ([JOLux](https://swiss.github.io/fedlex-jolux/introduction.html)). Germany's federal portal gesetze-im-internet.de shows only the current version [bk]. The new federal Rechtsinformationsportal is in a test phase, uses ELI and LegalDocML.de (an Akoma Ntoso variant), and has thin history before 2000 ([third-party docs](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@wolfgangihloff/rechtsinformationen-bund-de-mcp/blob/9cbfa2090d120d9ba47e6b5b09135704c58e0311/CLAUDE.md); [Bundestag 2021](https://www.bundestag.de/presse/hib/818262-818262)). The 16 Länder portals differ. This bears on the FAQ promise that "older projects get the law that applied then" (§8).

5. **EU law now forces cadastre, buildings and addresses open.** The High-Value Datasets regulation (EU) 2023/138 covers the INSPIRE themes administrative units, geographical names, addresses, buildings and cadastral parcels, with API and bulk access, applicable from June 2024 ([datos.gob.es](https://datos.gob.es/en/blog/complying-europe-inspire-and-high-value-geospatial-assemblies-regulation); [JRC](https://eurogeographics.org/app/uploads/2023/06/04.-20240116_Towards_spatial_and_open_data_discoverability_for_EU_Data_Spaces-JRC_ESCRIU.pdf)). Austria publishes the DKM and the address register under CC BY 4.0 ([BEV metadata](https://data.bev.gv.at/geonetwork/srv/api/records/ffaa7b22-c8b9-4e64-bcc2-1e003f52b9b0); [address register](https://inspire-geoportal.ec.europa.eu/srv/api/records/37d564f9-5d63-4760-aae6-29d3f98ee1b4)). German ALKIS is open in most Länder, with Bavaria the outlier ([FOSSGIS 2025](https://pretalx.com/fossgis2025/talk/TRJW9F/)). Switzerland is not in the EU but is open de facto through geo.admin.ch, geodienste.ch and cantonal OGD, with licence terms that vary by canton ([geodienste/OGD](https://ckan.opendata.swiss/en/dataset/amtliche-vermessung-zg)). Land registers (ownership, servitudes) stay behind fees or a legal-interest test in every country sampled.

6. **Licensing splits along source lines, not subject lines.** Austria's OIB guidelines, Switzerland's VKF fire rules and Germany's model rules (MBO, MVV TB, MLAR) are free [bk]. ÖNORM, DIN, SIA standards, the commentaries, BKI and CRB cost data are licensed. Some building-law knowledge can therefore be platform-curated, and some technical standards can never be (ticket 32). The "Normen vs. Gesetze" split tells you nothing about the licence. The licence is a separate attribute.

7. **The same word means different things in different countries.** An Austrian "Bebauungsplan" is the detailed plan under the Flächenwidmungsplan. In Germany "Bebauungsplan" is the binding plan and "Flächennutzungsplan" the preparatory one. Vienna's "Bauklasse" is a height class, while "Gebäudeklasse" (OIB, MBO) is a fire-safety class; both thresholds exist in both countries and are not the same. Terminology has to be data per jurisdiction, never shared vocabulary.

**Gap 2: the table mixes subject with form.** The "upload policy" column (upload possible / not needed / connection instead of upload) already says that an area's form and licence matter more to the product than its name. Row 15 is defined by its form (connection) and row 12 by its source (manufacturers). I recommend keeping the subject areas as a **classification of items** (a facet), and giving every item a **form** and a **licence class**:

- **Form:** `corpus` (text to retrieve and cite) · `reference` (structured, versioned data the agent computes with) · `connector` (live query against a public or account-bound service) · `configuration` (checklists, vocabularies, prompts, disclaimers, units).
- **Licence class:** `public-open` · `public-restricted` (fee per extract, legal-interest test, attribution or non-commercial terms) · `licensed-platform` (platform contracts the source, e.g. a future Austrian Standards deal) · `licensed-office` (the office's own licence, ticket 32) · `account-connection` (the office's login to a platform).

An area spans forms. Row 5 "Grundstück und Planungsrecht" is a cadastre connector, a zoning connector, a zoning-code reference list, a corpus of plan ordinances, and a configuration item ("in this Land, ask for the Bebauungsplan ordinance text if the GIS has no textual provisions").

## 3. Austria (live today; deepest)

**Authorities and competence.** The first instance is the mayor, acting in the Gemeinde's own sphere of competence. Appeals ran inside the municipality and now go to the Landesverwaltungsgericht. Vienna's building authority is the Magistrat (MA 37 Baupolizei). Statutory cities act through their Magistrat, and Länder can delegate to district authorities by ordinance [bk]. No public machine-readable competence directory exists. The usable keys are the Gemeindekennziffer (GKZ, whose first digit is the Land) from Statistik Austria's municipality register [bk] and a curated exceptions table. The model therefore needs an authority rule per Land with a small override list. A free-text directory would not serve.

**Professional practice.** Architects are Ziviltechniker under the ZTG 2019, organised in the Kammer der ZiviltechnikerInnen, with mandatory professional indemnity insurance [bk]. Building masters (Baumeister) under the GewO may also draw submission plans [bk], so "who may be Planverfasser" is a Land fact, not a profession constant. Fees: the binding fee ordinance (HOA) was withdrawn under competition law [bk]. The reference today is **LM.VM 2023** by Hans Lechner (first published 2014, re-issued 2023; Objektplanung Architektur 2nd ed. 15.09.2023), distributed by PMTools, with parts mirrored by the WKO ([WKO LM.VM](https://WKO.at/oe/gewerbe-handwerk/bau/02-lm.vm.2023-allgemeine-regelungen.pdf)). The chamber's competition recommendations build on LM.VM OA 2023 ([ZT-Kammer FAQ 2024](https://www.ztkammer.at/fileadmin/user_upload/redakteure_stk/03_Leistungen/Wettbewerbe/FAQs_Empfehlung_2024.pdf)). LM.VM defines service phases (LPH) and percentage weightings, which belong in reference data. "LM.VM (free)" in the team's table should be checked: the WKO PDFs exist, but the licence terms were not visible.

**Submission conventions.** Each Bauordnung lists the Einreichplan contents. Vienna's MA 37 publishes Merkblätter and checklists. Drawing standards are ÖNORM A 6240, CAD/BIM structure ÖNORM A 6241-1/-2 [bk]. Vienna ran BRISE, a BIM-based digital permit project [bk].

**Classification and measurement.** The OIB-RL definitions (Fluchtniveau, storeys, units, floor area to GK1-GK5) are the derivation ticket 30 names [bk]. ÖNORM B 1800 covers areas and volumes, ÖNORM B 1801-1 the cost structure [bk]. Vienna's Bauklasse I-VI (building height in the Bebauungsplan) is a separate concept.

**Change dynamics.** These matter for the edition model. The OIB-RL 2023 edition becomes binding Land by Land on different dates: Upper Austria from 1 Oct 2025 (RL 1-5 including the RL 1 guide) ([Oö. BauTV-Novelle 2025](https://land-oberoesterreich.gv.at/Mediendateien/Formulare/Dokumente%20VerfD/BauTV-Novelle_2025_-_VO_Text_samt_Erl%c3%a4uterungen.pdf)). Carinthia, Lower Austria, Upper Austria and Vienna were reported as adopters at an earlier, undated point ([forum-media](https://www.forum-media.at/news/Arbeitssicherheit-Brandschutz/OIB-Richtlinien-Aktuelle-Vorgaben-fuer-Betriebe-1259557?pk_kwd=203020-00)). **OIB-RL 6 has a 2025 edition** (EPBD 2024/1275 transposition, due 29 May 2026). Tyrol published it in LGBl. 59/2026 in July 2026 ([Tirol](https://www.tirol.gv.at/fileadmin/themen/bauen-wohnen/baupolizei/20260219_Zentrale_%C3%84nderungen_der_OIB_Richtlinie_6_im_Jahr_2025.pdf); [RIS LGBl](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/LgblAuth/LGBLA_TI_20260715_59/Anlage_59_TBV2026_Anlage6_KM.pdf)). A new **OIB-RL 7** (sustainable use of resources, FprEN 15978) and a re-edition of RL 1-6 are planned for 2027 ([WKO](https://www.wko.at/oe/gewerbe-handwerk/bau/20260930_aenderungsvorschlag_gs-bau_rl-7.pdf)). If the corpus holds only "OIB-RL May 2023", it is already incomplete for energy in at least Tyrol. That fits ticket 32a's freshness question. The real unit is "guideline edition × Land × date of binding force", not "guideline edition".

**Cost data.** Statistik Austria's Baupreisindex was rebased to 2025 from Q1 2026, and the separate Baukostenindex is published monthly with final values at T+75 days ([Q1 2026](https://www.statistik.at/fileadmin/announcement/2026/08/20260508Baupreisindex2026Q1.pdf); [Aug 2026](https://www.statistik.at/fileadmin/announcement/2026/09/20260915BaukostenindexAugust2026.pdf)). These are reference time series with a base year, so they need a versioned reference-data primitive.

**Terminology.** Austrian legal German: Baubewilligung, Bauanzeige, Einreichplan, Fluchtniveau, Geschoß, Stiege, Widmung. See §7.

## 4. Germany (sales start end of November 2026; near-term)

**Authorities and competence.** The untere Bauaufsichtsbehörde sits at the Landkreis or kreisfreie Stadt, in several Länder also at Große Kreisstädte or larger municipalities. The Gemeinde holds planning sovereignty (Bauleitplanung) and gives its Einvernehmen under §36 BauGB [bk]. Germany is the only DACH country with a **standardised competence data exchange**. XZuFi describes services, competences and locations. The Portalverbund Online-Gateway (PVOG) carries this data across Länder and resolves postal code to ARS (Regionalschlüssel), with a REST export at `/v2/verwaltungsobjekte` ([PVOG guide](https://produktportal.pvog.fitko.de/pdfs/BeD_Anbindungsanleitung.pdf); [FITKO 2026](https://www.it-planungsrat.de/fileadmin/it-planungsrat/der-it-planungsrat/fachkongress/fachkongress_2026/pr%C3%A4sentationen/Tag1_Deep_Dive_FIM_PVOG_Ott.pdf)). Commercial providers offer certified REST access ([TSA Infodienste](https://api.infodienste.de/fileadmin/Bibliothek/tsa.de/Schnittstellen_Dokumente/INFODIENSTE_REST_ZUFI_V4.pdf)). A Bauaufsicht connector can resolve ARS plus service key to the authority. Access is certified and keyed, not anonymous.

## 5. Switzerland (next market; four languages, 26 cantons)

**Authorities and competence.** The Gemeinde is the rule. The canton coordinates and approves anything outside building zones. Some cantons decide centrally: Geneva's Office des autorisations de construire [bk], Basel-Landschaft's Bauinspektorat ([GR overview](https://www.gr.ch:443/DE/institutionen/verwaltung/dvs/are/BAB/Baubewilligungsverfahren%20(BBV)_de.pdf)); Valais splits the dossiers ([VS](https://www.vs.ch/de/web/sajmte/bauverfahren)); Zurich routes cases needing cantonal review through a Leitstelle ([ZH](https://www.zh.ch/de/planen-bauen/baubewilligung/infos-baubehoerden-fachstellen/koordiniertes-verfahren.html)). Portals: eBau in BE (mandatory digital since 2022), AG (per municipality) ([AG eBau](https://www.ag.ch/ebau)). Keys: the BFS municipality number. The BFS keeps a historicised municipality register with mutations [bk], which matters because Swiss municipalities merge often and a project's Stichtag can predate a merger.

**Public data services.** Switzerland is the most mature of the three.

SIA 102 covers services and fees. KBOB publishes fee recommendations for public clients. Professional regulation is cantonal: several French- and Italian-speaking cantons and Lucerne restrict who may submit (via the REG register), while most German-speaking cantons do not [bk]. Verify per canton. This is a direct analogue of Germany's Bauvorlageberechtigung and must be the same item kind.

**Submission and BIM.** Cantonal Baugesuch requirement lists; the Bauprofil/Baugespann (staking) is a Swiss procedural specific [bk]; SIA 400 drawing standards; SIA 2051 BIM [bk].

**Languages.** Federal law is in three equally binding languages. Bilingual cantons are FR, VS and BE (de/fr). GR is trilingual (de/it/rm). A municipality can be bilingual (Biel/Bienne) [bk]. The answer language (the office's language) and the source language (the binding text's language) therefore differ. Where several languages are equally authoritative, the agent may cite whichever matches the answer language. Where only one language is authoritative, it must cite that one, and an English or German translation is never authoritative.

## 6. Samples: France, England, Netherlands, California, and two neighbours

**France.** The permit (permis de construire) is issued by the maire, in the commune's name where a PLU or a carte communale gives the commune permit competence, otherwise in the State's (Code de l'urbanisme L422-1, with a transitional rule for pending applications) [verify]. Since 1 Jan 2022 every commune must accept electronic filing, and those over 3,500 inhabitants must also process electronically through PLAT'AU. By 2023 about 40% of files ran through PLAT'AU ([réunion DEAL](https://www.reunion.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/pc_en_ligne_-_quelles_sont_mes_obligations_legales_au_1er_janvier_2022_octobre_2021_.pdf); [Maire-info](https://www.maire-info.com/urbanisme-ama©nagement/permis-de-construire-en-ligne-letat-rappelle-�-lordre-ses-propres-services-article-27858)). Zoning: the national Géoportail de l'Urbanisme, with PLU data under the CNIG standard v2024-01 ([CNIG](https://cnig.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/231220_standard_cnig_plu_v2024-01.pdf)). That is one national schema, unlike Austria's nine or Germany's XPlanung-plus-PDF. Addresses: the Base Adresse Nationale API, moved to IGN's Géoplateforme, with the old domain redirecting from January 2026 ([data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/posts/lapi-adresse-de-la-base-adresse-nationale-est-transferee-a-lign-10/)). Risks: the Géorisques API (BRGM) [bk]. Cadastre: open PCI vector data (Etalab) [bk]. Heritage: Mérimée plus the 500 m "abords" of historic monuments, where the Architecte des Bâtiments de France must consent [bk]. Law: Légifrance (LEGI with article versions, API via PISTE) [bk]. Practice: an architect is mandatory above 150 m² floor area for private individuals; Ordre des architectes; the public-works mission phases ESQ, APS, APD, PRO, ACT, VISA, DET, AOR sit in the Code de la commande publique; no fee scale [bk]. Measurement: surface de plancher and emprise au sol (Code de l'urbanisme). Forms: numbered Cerfa forms with numbered attachments (PCMI1…) [bk]. **Pattern:** France proves "country = one schema", and Cerfa attachment lists are a natural "submission requirements" reference dataset.

**England (UK).** Planning permission comes from the local planning authority, building control from the local authority or a registered approver. For higher-risk buildings (during design and construction: 18 m+ or 7+ storeys with 2+ residential units, or a qualifying hospital or care home; the occupation regime differs) the **Building Safety Regulator** runs gateways ([gov.uk criteria](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/criteria-for-determining-whether-a-new-building-that-is-being-designed-and-constructed-is-a-higher-risk-building)). Its statutory clock is 8 weeks for existing and 12 for new buildings, but Gateway 2 averaged 22 weeks in early 2025. Staged applications came in late 2025, and the BSR became a standalone body on 27 Jan 2026 ([Trowers](https://www.trowers.com/insights/2025/april/managing-delays-in-the-gateways-process); [RIBA](https://www.riba.org/work/insights-and-resources/professional-features/building-safety-act-professional-features/what-can-the-latest-building-safety-regulator-figures-for-gateway-2-applications-tell-architects/); [Shoosmiths](https://shoosmiths.com/perspectives/stories/articles/building-safety-developments-expected-in-2026)). So the competent authority depends on a classification fact, as in Switzerland. Data: **planning.data.gov.uk**, an open API with 100+ datasets (conservation areas, listed buildings, Article 4 areas, tree preservation zones) queryable by point or UPRN, no authentication ([docs](https://www.planning.data.gov.uk/docs)). This is the best single-call location connector in the sample. HM Land Registry INSPIRE Index Polygons are under OGL from July 2020 and refreshed monthly, but are indicative only; title registers cost a fee per copy ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/inspire-index-polygons-spatial-data); [HMLR spec](https://use-land-property-data.service.gov.uk/datasets/nps/tech-spec/1?back=true)). Law: legislation.gov.uk with point-in-time versions [bk]. The Approved Documents are statutory guidance, a third category between statute and norm. Practice: RIBA Plan of Work 2020 stages 0-7; ARB protects the title "architect"; no fee scale; NRM (RICS) for measurement, BCIS for costs (licensed); Uniclass 2015; UK BIM Framework [bk]. **Pattern:** the UK is four legal jurisdictions in one state (England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland have separate building regulations) [bk]. "Country" in the model must not be the unit of law. Jurisdiction is.

**Netherlands.** The Omgevingswet has been in force since 1 Jan 2024 and bundles 26 instrument types published through the national DSO-LV. Its Ozon component exposes APIs (start with the "presenteren" API), published under the STOP/TPOD standards. From 1 Jan 2026 municipalities must amend the omgevingsplan in STOP/TPOD only (TAM-IMRO phased out) ([RPnl phase-out](https://ruimtelijkeplannen.nl/web-roo/docs/Uitfasering_services_en_diensten_RPnl_en_alternatieven_vanuit-DSO-LV.pdf); [IPLO standards](https://iplo.nl/digitaal-stelsel/aansluiten/standaarden/introductie-standaarden-omgevingswet)). This is the clearest example in the sample of **law and map as one object**: a "rule on the map" is a text provision with a geometry and an activity. Technical rules: Bbl. Quality assurance: Wkb (private quality assurer for consequence class 1) [bk]. Registers: BAG addresses and buildings are open via PDOK; BRK cadastral extracts follow the Kadaster tariff ([consultation](https://internetconsultatie.nl/tarievenbagbgt/document/6274)). Heritage: the RCE Rijksmonumentenregister (open) [bk]. Law: wetten.overheid.nl with BWB identifiers and versions [bk]. Practice: the architect title is protected (Architectenregister), DNR 2011 is the client-consultant contract, NEN 2580 covers measurement, NL-SfB classification [bk].

**California (US).** Permits come from the city or county building department. The 2025 California Building Standards Code (Title 24) took effect statewide on **1 Jan 2026** for applications from that date. The state offers free view-only access, and ICC sells the editions ([BSC Bulletin 25-01](https://www.dgs.ca.gov/-/media/Divisions/BSC/06-News/Information-Bulletins/2025/BSC-Bulletin-25-01-FINAL.pdf); [DGS](https://www.dgs.ca.gov/bsc/codes)). The free version is viewable but not indexable, which is ticket 32's licensing pattern for statutory text. Zoning codes are municipal, often hosted by private publishers. Parcels come per county assessor (APN), with no state cadastre. FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer is available as open WMS/REST, paged by the service's own record limit ([NFHL service](https://hazards.fema.gov/arcgis/rest/services/public/NFHL/MapServer)). Seismic and wind design values come from ASCE 7 via the ASCE Hazard Tool (partly paid), and CAL FIRE publishes fire hazard severity zones [bk]. CEQA covers environmental review. Practice: the California Architects Board licenses architects; AIA phases (SD, DD, CD, bidding, CA) and AIA contract documents (licensed); RSMeans (licensed); imperial units [bk]. **Pattern:** units and number formats are per-jurisdiction configuration, and a three-year code cycle tied to a statewide effective date is the simplest Stichtag rule in the sample.

**Liechtenstein** [bk]: one national building law and one national building authority (no municipal permit level), EEA member, largely uses SIA norms and Swiss practice. A small jurisdiction whose authority chain ends at the state. **South Tyrol** [bk]: an Italian autonomous province with its own planning law (LG 9/2018 "Raum und Landschaft"). The Gemeinde issues permits under Italian national building law (DPR 380/2001) and structural norms (NTC 2018). It keeps the Austrian-style land register (Grundbuch/tavolare). Official texts are in German and Italian (Ladin regionally). Rules on which language prevails must be verified. South Tyrol shows why "country" cannot be the language unit or the land-register-system unit.

### 8.2 Unifying identifiers and standards

- **Jurisdiction:** ISO 3166-1/-2 (AT-9, DE-BY, CH-ZH) and Eurostat NUTS/LAU, plus the national municipality codes: GKZ (AT), AGS/ARS (DE), BFS number (CH), INSEE code (FR), CBS gemeentecode (NL), GSS (UK), FIPS/GEOID (US) [bk]. Municipal codes change with mergers, so jurisdiction needs validity periods too. The Swiss historicised register is the model to copy.
- **Parcels:** KG + Gst-Nr (AT), Flurstückskennzeichen (DE), EGRID (CH), IDU (FR), kadastrale aanduiding (NL), title number / INSPIRE ID (England), APN (US) [bk]. **Buildings and addresses:** AGWR OBJ-ID and Adresscode (AT), EGID/EDID (CH), BAG IDs (NL), UPRN (UK), BAN id (FR) [bk]. Germany has no national building ID.
- **Geodata:** INSPIRE themes (CP, AD, BU, PLU, NZ, PS) with the HVD obligations; OGC WMS/WFS and OGC API Features; ISO 19152 LADM; INTERLIS (CH, the ÖREB framework model) [bk]; XPlanung (DE, maps to INSPIRE PLU) ([GDI-BW](https://geoportal-bw.de/documents/d/guest/inspire-konforme-bereitstellung-von-bebauungsplanen-der-gdi-bw_08-2016)); CNIG (FR); STOP/TPOD (NL).
- **Law:** ELI (EU register, ontology v1.5 2024) ([EUR-Lex ELI](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli-register/benefits.html)) used by Fedlex and the German portal; JOLux (CH); Akoma Ntoso / LegalDocML.de; ECLI for case law [bk]. ELI's "work / expression / manifestation" levels and date-of-applicability fields correspond to ticket 30's "Ausgabe" and validity period. They are the standard's names for the same thing.
- **Building information:** IFC 4.3 = ISO 16739-1:2024 ([buildingSMART](https://www.buildingsmart.org/?p=29521)); IDS 1.0, the official buildingSMART standard since June 2024 ([buildingSMART](https://www.buildingsmart.org/?p=30065)), is the right format for "what a submission model must contain" as machine-checkable reference; bSDD, a service with 100+ dictionaries rather than a standard ([buildingSMART](https://www.buildingsmart.org/?p=28515)), for jurisdiction-scoped properties and terms; BCF; ISO 19650; ISO 12006-2 classification (Uniclass, eBKP-H, DIN 276 as national instances) [bk].
- **Procedure and competence:** XZuFi/FIM (DE), XBau (DE), eCH standards (CH) [bk]. No cross-country standard exists for competence data, so ours should borrow XZuFi's split of service, competence and area.
- **Catalogues:** DCAT-AP for describing the sources themselves (provider, licence, update frequency, endpoint) [bk]. That gives the "provenance and freshness" record of each connector a standard shape.

### 8.3 Granularity

Law sits at nation, state/canton and municipality. Geodata sits at parcel. Holidays sit at state, canton and sometimes municipality. Procurement thresholds sit at the EU. Authority competence sits at municipality or district, and in CH and England also depends on a fact. Language sits at municipality in CH. Land-register systems follow history, not borders (South Tyrol). **No single "country" level carries all of these.** The model needs a jurisdiction graph (EU → country → state/canton → district → municipality, plus special areas such as the Swiss non-building zone or a German Sanierungsgebiet), and each item must say at which node it applies.

Two consequences for the primitives:

- **Facts and their resolvers belong in country base knowledge, not in the setup flow.** "Building class" in Austria resolves from Fluchtniveau, storeys, units and area under OIB-RL 2023 or 2025. "Outside building zone" in Switzerland resolves from the ÖREB extract and changes the authority. If these live in reference data per jurisdiction and edition, a new region needs no change to the setup flow, which is ticket 30's last criterion.
- **The address-to-authority-to-Stichtag chain crosses three forms.** Address is a connector, authority is a reference rule (with a connector in DE), Stichtag is a reference rule citing corpus. One primitive set must express all three with the same versioning and provenance, or ticket 31 ends up hand-coded per country.

## 10. What office base knowledge (ticket 32) depends on

- **The licence decides who may hold the text, not the kind of item.** OIB-RL, VKF and MBO/MVV TB are public, so the platform can hold them. ÖNORM, DIN and SIA are licensed, so the office supplies them until a platform deal exists (Austrian Standards talks; DIN Media and the SIA shop are the counterparts in DE and CH [bk]). The same item (say "ÖNORM B 1600, edition 2017") can be a platform reference entry without text, plus an office-owned text expression visible only to that office.
- **Authority directives are scoped to one authority node in the jurisdiction graph** (MA 37 = Vienna's building authority). An office directive applies only when the project's resolved authority matches. That requires the ticket 31 chain to produce an authority identifier, not a name.
- **Freshness.** The OIB-RL 6 2025 edition shows how fast a platform corpus goes stale (published by Tyrol in July 2026; others to follow by the May 2026 deadline or later). Change monitoring has concrete hooks: the RIS change feed (SOAP), Fedlex SPARQL modification dates, DIBt/Bauministerkonferenz publication pages, the OIB website, Statistik Austria release calendars. Each connector's DCAT-style record should carry an expected update frequency so "last checked" and "may be stale" are computed the same way for platform and office items.

## 11. The two FAQ claims, from the external side

- *"For Austria and Germany the most important law texts are in."* Everything needed for Germany is publicly obtainable: federal law via gesetze-im-internet.de and the new portal, Länder law via 16 portals, the MBO/MVV TB/MLAR models, and DIBt zone tables. But the team's own table records none of it as integrated, and nothing external changes that. Until LBO, VV TB, BauGB, BauNVO and BauVorlV for at least the sales-target Länder are in, the claim is not true for Germany.
- *"Because we keep older versions, older projects get the law that applied then."* For Austria this is feasible: RIS serves point-in-time consolidated versions, though Länder history starts between 2011 and 2016, and the authentic Landesgesetzblätter cover earlier years. It holds only if the product stores editions with validity periods (ticket 30). Ticket 30 says it does not yet. For Germany it is harder: federal history only through the new portal, mostly after 2000; Länder history inconsistent. A per-jurisdiction "history available from" date belongs in the source record, so the agent can say when it cannot answer for a past Stichtag.
