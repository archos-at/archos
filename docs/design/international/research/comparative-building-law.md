# R1: How the law an architect deals with is structured, across jurisdictions

Comparative research for a jurisdiction-agnostic model of country base knowledge.
Research date: 2026-11-04. Status: external research input, not a design. Facts carry their source and date; where a source was secondary or the state could not be confirmed, the text says so.

---

## 0. Why this research, and what it has to serve

The foundation (Notion ticket 30, "Grundlagen wissen, für welches Projekt und welchen Zeitpunkt sie gelten") has two primary consumers. Ticket 31 is project setup ("Projekt anlegen: in einem Durchgang, in der Zeit verortet"). Ticket 32 is the office overlay ("Büroeigene Normen, Kommentare und Weisungen nutzbar machen"). I read all three tickets on 2026-11-04, plus the process card "B2 Baurechtlicher Projektsteckbrief". The research is aimed at the questions those tickets cannot answer without knowing how law is built in other countries:

- Ticket 31 wants a verified address to resolve "Land, Bundesland und Gemeinde". Abroad, the chain is not always three levels, and the competent authority is often not the municipality. **Section 4.1.**
- Ticket 31 wants the reference date (Stichtag) chosen "nachvollziehbar" from submission, permit and project start. Which date counts differs by jurisdiction, by instrument, and by procedure type, and some transitions give the applicant a choice. **Section 4.4.**
- Ticket 30 wants every text to record "wo ein Text einen anderen verbindlich macht, verdrängt oder davon abweicht". That is the bindingness relation. Every jurisdiction has it, but it works differently in each. **Sections 4.2–4.3.**
- Ticket 30 wants per-region decisive facts, the document they usually appear in, and what to request. Ticket 31 wants public location sources searched and the findings put to the person with their source. In several countries the land-use plan is a GIS dataset, and sometimes that dataset is the legally effective instrument. **Section 4.8.**
- Ticket 31 wants the classification of the existing building against the classification after the work. Classification schemes and their measurement inputs are national data. **Sections 4.5–4.6.**
- Ticket 32 says the platform may not index licensed ÖNORMs, and offices must be able to fill that gap only for themselves. Standards licensing follows the same pattern in every country examined. **Section 4.9.**

Two vocabulary decisions from the tickets are kept here. **Edition (Ausgabe)** is the state of a source, such as "OIB-Richtlinie 2, Ausgabe Mai 2023". **Fassung** is the state of an office's uploaded file. Edition is used throughout.

## 1. Findings in brief

1. **No country runs on a single legal hierarchy.** Every jurisdiction examined layers supranational, national, subnational and municipal law. On top of that, *functional* competences cut across the territorial units: England's Building Safety Regulator for higher-risk buildings, German lower building authorities at county level, Swiss cantonal approval of municipal zoning. Territory and competence are two separate graphs.
2. **"Model code, then adoption with amendments" is the dominant way building rules spread.** Austria (OIB-Richtlinien), Germany (MBO, MVV TB), Switzerland (IVHB, VKF fire regulations, MuKEn energy rules) and the USA (ICC I-codes) all work this way. Adoption is partial, lagged and amended. As of October 2026, three of nine Austrian Länder have **not** adopted the OIB-Richtlinien 2023 at all, and two more adopted only Richtlinien 1–5 ([OIB, Inkrafttreten table, Stand Oktober 2026](https://www.oib.or.at/de/kernaufgaben/oib-richtlinien)). An adoption is a first-class fact with its own date, scope and deviations. It is not an attribute of the model.
3. **Bindingness belongs to a relation, not to a document.** The same ÖNORM or DIN standard can be declared binding in one Land, referenced as guidance in another, and appear in a third only as a contract term. The data has to record *who* makes *what* binding, *how* (declared binding, incorporated by reference, presumption of conformity, recognised rules of technology, contract incorporation), *for which edition*, and *with which deviations*.
4. **Three time axes, not one.** (a) The interval in which an edition is in force. (b) The rule that picks which edition applies to a given project (the Stichtag rule). (c) When the platform or office last verified the edition. Scotland picks the Technical Handbook edition by the date the building warrant application was submitted ([gov.scot, 2026](https://www.gov.scot/publications/building-standards-technical-handbook-domestic-april-2026-pdf/)). Saxony uses start of works for exempted projects and date of grant for permit procedures ([Sachsen SMR, Eurocode Stichtag](https://bauen-wohnen.sachsen.de/download/an_PI_Aender_bauaufs_Einf_ECs_Stichtag.pdf)). Sweden let the developer choose old or new rules until 30 June 2026, on condition that the whole chosen set was applied ([Boverket](https://www.boverket.se/en/start/laws-and-regulations/national-regulations/building-regulations/)). The Stichtag rule is per-jurisdiction data, and it can carry options and sunsets.
5. **Land-use law is increasingly spatial data, and sometimes the dataset is the instrument.** In France, since 1 January 2023, a PLU becomes enforceable only once it is published on the Géoportail de l'urbanisme ([collectivites-locales.gouv.fr](https://www.collectivites-locales.gouv.fr/index.php/FAQ/la-publicite-des-documents-durbanisme)). Switzerland runs a cadastre of public-law property restrictions (ÖREB-Kataster) in all cantons. Germany made XPlanung binding from 1 February 2023. The Netherlands puts the omgevingsplan "on the map" through the DSO. A connector to such a source returns *law*, not just context.
6. **Classification schemes look alike but are not equivalent.** Austrian and German "Gebäudeklasse 1–5" share a name but not their thresholds or their measurement basis. US IBC (occupancy group × construction type), French ERP type/category, UK purpose groups and Swiss VKF height classes are different shapes altogether. The data must store schemes, their inputs and their derivation rules per jurisdiction, and never one universal "building class".
7. **Measurement rules are national standards that classification depends on.** ÖNORM B 1800, DIN 277, SIA 416, NEN 2580, and the French "surface de plancher" defined in the Code de l'urbanisme all differ. A derived value has to name the measurement rule it used.
8. **The licensing split is the same everywhere: statutes are open, standards are paywalled, and the law is pushing back on the paywall.** Austria's NormG 2016 § 9 requires declared-binding national standards to be freely accessible. The CJEU held in C-588/21 P (5 March 2024) that harmonised standards form part of EU law and access must be granted. The US D.C. Circuit held non-commercial posting of incorporated standards to be fair use (12 Sept 2023). For ingestion, standards appear as *referenced items without text* at platform level, and as *licensed text* in the office layer (ticket 32).
9. **Official identifiers are converging on ELI for legislation and ECLI for case law.** Akoma Ntoso / LegalDocML is the structured full-text format in Switzerland (since 30 May 2022), the UK and German federal law (LegalDocML.de). Austria's RIS returns ELI. Standards have no common identifier beyond "designation:edition-date".
10. **Language authority is per jurisdiction and per instrument.** Swiss federal law is equally authentic in German, French and Italian. In South Tyrol, under Art. 99 of the Autonomy Statute (DPR 670/1972, as I recall the text; not re-verified online), the Italian wording prevails for legislative acts. A translation is never authoritative, and "which language is authoritative" is data.
11. **Advice regulation is per jurisdiction and fairly strict in Germany.** The German BGH held that an architect representing clients in objection proceedings (I ZR 227/19, 11 Feb 2021) and supplying a self-drafted contract clause (VII ZR 190/22, 9 Nov 2023) was not a permitted ancillary legal service under § 5 RDG.

## 2. DACH in depth

### 2.1 Austria (live today)

**Jurisdiction.** Federal state with nine Länder. Under Art. 15 B-VG, building law (Baurecht) and spatial planning (Raumordnung) are Land matters. That gives nine Bauordnungen, nine Raumordnungsgesetze and nine Bautechnikverordnungen or equivalents. Municipalities act in their "eigener Wirkungsbereich": the mayor (Bürgermeister) is usually the building authority of first instance, but the Land law names it, with exceptions: in Graz it is the Stadtsenat (§ 2 Stmk. BauG), in Upper Austria's statutory cities the Magistrat (§ 55 Oö. BauO 1994), and a Land can transfer the local building police to a state authority by ordinance; in most Länder appeals go to the municipal council or Gemeindevorstand and then to the Landesverwaltungsgericht; where a Land excludes the municipal Instanzenzug in building matters (Styria, for example), the complaint is filed with the issuing authority and decided directly by the Landesverwaltungsgericht (§ 12 VwGVG). The route is per Land [verify each]. Vienna is Land and municipality at once, and the Magistrat (MA 37) is the building authority. Federal law reaches architects through other channels: the procedural code (AVG), procurement (BVergG 2018), the Ziviltechnikergesetz 2019 for professional law, the BauKG for site safety coordination, energy-performance-certificate law (EAVG) plus the federal transposition of EU law, and the Denkmalschutzgesetz, because monument protection is federal (Bundesdenkmalamt). Heritage therefore runs on a *federal* track beside the *Land* building procedure: changing a listed monument needs the Bundesdenkmalamt's approval as well as the building permit, in two separate procedures, and the work may start only once both are granted (bda.gv.at, Häufige Fragen). That is the first example of overlapping jurisdictions.

**Model code and adoption.** The OIB-Richtlinien 1–6 (mechanical resistance, fire, hygiene/health/environment, safety in use and accessibility, sound, energy) are an intergovernmental model. The OIB General Assembly adopts them, and each Land declares them binding in its own law, usually in a Bautechnikverordnung, with deviations. The OIB's own adoption table (as of October 2026):

| Land | OIB-RL 2023 (1–5) | OIB-RL 2023 (6) | OIB-RL 6 Ausgabe 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burgenland | – | – | – |
| Kärnten | 31 Dec 2024 | 31 Dec 2024 | – |
| Niederösterreich | 18 Mar 2025 | 18 Mar 2025 | – |
| Oberösterreich | 1 Oct 2025 | – | – |
| Salzburg | 6 Aug 2026 | – | – |
| Steiermark | – | – | – |
| Tirol | 16 Jul 2026 | – | 16 Jul 2026 |
| Vorarlberg | – | – | – |
| Wien | 23 Feb 2024 | 23 Feb 2024 | 15 Jul 2026 |

Source: [OIB, Inkrafttreten der OIB-Richtlinien](https://www.oib.or.at/de/kernaufgaben/oib-richtlinien), fetched 2026-11-04. The 2023 edition was adopted on 25 May 2023, and OIB-RL 6 Ausgabe 2025 on 29 August 2025. The OIB notes that Land law may add transitional provisions, exceptions and deviations.

Three consequences follow. First, on 4 November 2026 Styria has adopted OIB 2019, Upper Austria OIB 2023 for RL 1–5 but OIB 2019 for RL 6, and Vienna OIB 2023 for RL 1–5 and RL 6 Ausgabe 2025. Second, an edition can be adopted *per Richtlinie*, not per package. Third, Tirol skipped RL 6 2023 and went straight to 2025. Which adopted edition governs a given project follows the transitional provisions, so a Vienna procedure pending when the 2026 amendment took effect keeps the previous law [verify]. The unit of adoption is therefore (instrument edition × adopting jurisdiction × date × scope × deviations).

**Standards.** ÖNORMs, published by Austrian Standards International, are voluntary unless a law or ordinance declares them binding (NormG 2016 § 9). Under the Wirtschaftskammer's reading of § 9, purely Austrian standards that are declared binding must be made accessible free of charge. In practice access is still restricted, and a 2020 Lower Austrian motion criticised the fact that binding ÖNORMs are not freely online ([WKO](https://www.wko.at/oe/news/winter-2015.pdf); [NÖ Landtag 993](https://noe-landtag.gv.at/fileadmin/gegenstaende/19/09/993/993A.pdf); [NormG on jusline](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/normg)). Most ÖNORMs reach a project through *reference* from OIB-Richtlinien (the OIB rules cite ÖNORM B 1600 for accessibility and ÖNORM B 8110 for thermal protection, for example), through contract (ÖNORM B 2110 as general contract conditions for works), or as recognised rules of technology in civil liability. Ticket 32 records the practical failure: feld72's first real test case needed three or four ÖNORMs that the catalogue lists without text.

**Procedures and dates.** Each Bauordnung defines its own procedure types, such as Baubewilligung, Bauanzeige, vereinfachtes Verfahren and bewilligungsfreie Vorhaben. The AVG sets a six-month decision period (§ 73), and deadlines are computed under §§ 32–33 AVG: when the last day falls on a weekend or public holiday, the deadline moves to the next working day. Transitional provisions usually freeze pending procedures under the old law. NÖ BO 2014 § 70 says proceedings pending on the day of entry into force are "nach der bisherigen Rechtslage zu Ende zu führen" ([gesetzefinden.at](https://gesetzefinden.at/landesrecht/landesgesetze/no--bo-2014/para-70)). Vienna's 2023 amendment made certain stricter demolition rules apply to applications *filed after* 1 July 2023, a date *before* the amendment entered into force (MA 37 letter on the draft, [WKO](https://www.wko.at/wien/information-consulting/immobilien-vermoegenstreuhaender/827777-2023-abbruchansuchen-wirtschaftliche-abbruchreife.pdf)). The trigger event, filing versus pending versus decision, is chosen per amendment and sometimes per provision.

**Land use.** Each Land's Raumordnungsgesetz has municipalities issue an örtliches Entwicklungskonzept, a Flächenwidmungsplan and Bebauungspläne. Formats run from Land GIS viewers to PDF scans. There is no national machine-readable standard like XPlanung. Hazard zone plans (Gefahrenzonenpläne, from the forest service "die.wildbach" and federal water management) are a separate federal-origin layer.

**Classification and measurement.** OIB defines Gebäudeklassen GK 1–5 by escape level (Fluchtniveau), number of storeys above ground, number of use units and gross floor area (OIB Begriffsbestimmungen). Ticket 30 already names this derivation. ÖNORM B 1800 is the area and volume measurement standard.

**Professional practice and fees.** Architects are Ziviltechniker under the ZTG 2019, organised in the Kammer der Ziviltechniker:innen. Binding fee schedules were abandoned on competition grounds. Offices calculate with non-binding performance and fee models, which I could not confirm as of 2026; treat them as office-overlay material, not platform law.

**Legal data.** RIS offers an OGD REST interface (secondary sources name `data.bka.gv.at/ris/api/v2.6`), keyless, licensed CC BY 4.0, with ELI identifiers on federal gazette items, consolidated Land law selectable per Land, and a change-history query. The RIS netiquette asks for 1–2 s between pages and bulk access outside office hours ([at-ris-mcp on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/at-ris-mcp/), secondary; official specification not retrieved). OIB-Richtlinien are published by the OIB as PDFs, not in RIS.

**Language.** German. Austrian usage differs from German in exactly the words the product handles: "Geschoß" next to "Geschoss", "Widmung", "Bauwerber", "Einreichung", "Bescheid", "Ziviltechniker".

### 2.2 Germany

**Jurisdiction.** The Bund holds planning law (BauGB, BauNVO, PlanZV) and energy law (GEG, replaced by the GModG in 2026, see below). The 16 Länder hold building regulation (Landesbauordnungen), monument protection (16 Denkmalschutzgesetze), architects' law (16 Architektengesetze and chambers) and the technical building rules. Municipalities hold planning sovereignty (Planungshoheit) and issue Flächennutzungspläne and Bebauungspläne as by-laws (Satzungen). The building authority is usually the *untere Bauaufsichtsbehörde* of the county or the county-free city, so for most municipalities the authority is **not** the municipality. The municipality still joins the procedure through its consent under § 36 BauGB (Einvernehmen), which is deemed given after two months.

**Model and adoption.** The Bauministerkonferenz maintains the Musterbauordnung (MBO), and each Land adapts it into its LBO. Technical building rules follow the same two-step pattern. The DIBt publishes the **MVV TB**, a model administrative provision listing technical building rules, many of them DIN or EN standards with deviations. Current edition 2025/1, published 20 May 2025, with correction notices of 29 July and 22 October 2025. Each Land then introduces its own VV TB or VwV TB:

- Lower Saxony's VV TB, "Fassung November 2025", is based on MVV TB 2025/1 ([AK Niedersachsen PDF](https://www.aknds.de/fileadmin/aknds/PDFs/Aktuell-Seite/VV_TB_20.11.2025_mbl-2025-550.pdf)).
- Saarland adopts 2025/1 subject to its own deviations under number 3 ([umwelt-online](https://umwelt-online.de/recht/bau/laender/saarland/z25_1029.htm)).
- Baden-Württemberg's VwV TB of 5 February 2025 follows the earlier MVV TB 2024/1 ([AKBW Merkblatt 611](https://akbw.fuf.de/fileadmin/download/dokumenten_datenbank/AKBW_Merkblaetter/Baurecht_Planungsrecht/Merkblatt611_VwV-TB-2025.pdf)).

So the same project type built in Hannover and in Stuttgart in early 2026 could fall under different editions of the federal model. This is the Austrian OIB pattern again, one level deeper, because the MVV TB itself references DIN editions.

**How standards become binding.** Three routes. (1) A *Technische Baubestimmung* listed in the Land's VV TB is binding under the LBO (MBO § 85a). Deviation is allowed if equivalent performance is shown. (2) The *allgemein anerkannte Regeln der Technik* (a.a.R.d.T.) are the civil-law standard of care in works contracts (§ 633 BGB). DIN standards are presumed to reflect them, but the presumption can be rebutted. (3) Contract: VOB/B (DIN 1961), and VOB/C (the ATV DIN 18299 ff.) through it. "Gebäudetyp E", the push to let parties contract below the a.a.R.d.T., is the live controversy. The BMK in November 2025 urged the Bund to change the civil-law frame and noted the federal law is not expected before the end of 2026, taking effect in 2027 ([StMB Bayern, 147. BMK](https://stmb.bayern.de/med/aktuell/archiv/2025/251121bmk/index.php)). For the data model this matters because the binding *mode* of the same DIN standard changes with the legal route.

**Planning law.** BauGB (Bauleitplanung, §§ 30–35 admissibility: inside a Bebauungsplan, inside built-up areas, outer area), BauNVO (types of building area, dimension of use: GRZ, GFZ, BMZ). An important temporal subtlety: the BauNVO edition that applies is the one in force when *the Bebauungsplan* was adopted, not the one in force at permit date. A 1968 plan is read with BauNVO 1968. The reference date of a plan-derived rule therefore attaches to the *plan*, not to the project. Recent change: the "Bau-Turbo" law entered into force on **30 October 2025**. It created § 246e BauGB, an experimental clause allowing deviation from planning law for housing, subject to the municipality's consent under the new § 36a with a consent fiction, and **limited to 31 December 2030** ([GÖRG, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.goerg.de/de/aktuelles/veroeffentlichungen/20-11-2025/bau-turbo-gesetz-in-kraft-schaffung-von-mehr-wohnraum-leicht-gemacht); [Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/de/en/who-we-are/news/press-media/newsletters/newsletter-immobilienrecht/newsletter-update-immobilienrecht-1-2026/bau-turbo-ist-in-kraft-getreten)). A rule with a built-in sunset date is a validity interval with a *known* end, which the model must support.

**Land-use data.** XPlanung is the binding exchange standard for land-use and landscape plans, decided by the IT-Planungsrat and binding since 1 February 2023 ([Stadt Münster](https://www.stadt-muenster.de/stadtplanung/bauleitplanung)). Coverage of legacy plans is uneven. Many older Bebauungspläne exist only as scanned PDFs.

**Energy.** The GEG 2020/2023 was replaced by the **Gebäudemodernisierungsgesetz (GModG)**. Cabinet adopted it on 13 May 2026, Bundestag and Bundesrat on 10 July 2026, promulgated as BGBl. 2026 I Nr. 226 (issued 28 July 2026), with the main provisions in force from 29 July 2026 according to secondary sources. It drops the 65 % renewable-heating rule and transposes the recast EPBD ([iww](https://www.iww.de/pbp/architektenrecht/gesetzgebung-das-gebaeudemodernisierungsgesetz-ist-beschlossen-und-kann-in-kraft-treten-n173832); [Ebner Stolz](https://www.ebnerstolz.de/de/unser-angebot/leistungen/rechtsberatung/energierecht/gebaeudemodernisierungsgesetz-verabschiedet-109427.html)). Secondary sources disagree on staged dates, one naming 1 January 2027, so check the BGBl text before ingesting. A whole statute replaced by a successor under a new name is the "supersedes" relation in its starkest form.

**Procurement.** GWB part 4 with the VgV above EU thresholds (planning services: the VgV chapter on architects' and engineers' services). Below thresholds, UVgO or VOB/A depending on the Land. EU thresholds from 1 January 2026: €216,000 for services of sub-central authorities, €140,000 for central government, €5,404,000 for works (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2152, [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2025/2152)). Thresholds change every two years by delegated act. They are *time-versioned reference values*, not text.

**Fees and phases.** HOAI 2021, with Leistungsphasen 1–9 and fee tables that have been non-binding since CJEU C-377/17 (4 July 2019). The phase model is reference data that many other things key on, such as Leistungsbilder, deliverables and cost determination under DIN 276.

**Professional law.** The Bauvorlageberechtigung (MBO § 65 and its LBO equivalents) defines who may sign a permit submission: architects on the chamber list, listed engineers, and in some Länder master craftsmen for small buildings. Details differ by Land. In 2022 the BMK agreed with the European Commission to amend §§ 65 ff. MBO (a "restricted" entitlement), and the chambers opposed it ([BayIKa/BAK letter, March 2023](https://www.bayika.de/bayika-wAssets/docs/aktuelles/2023/2023-03-13_BIngK_BAK_Schreiben-ASBW-Maerz-2023.pdf)). Whether each Land has implemented it could not be verified.

**Site safety.** BaustellV 1998 (SiGeKo, the health and safety coordinator) implements Directive 92/57/EEC. Austria's BauKG implements the same directive.

**Legal data.** The federal NeuRIS service (rechtsinformationen.bund.de) has been in public test since April 2025. It has an open API, ELI identifiers (e.g. `eli/bund/bgbl-1/2017/s2097/2025-01-01/1/deu`), LegalDocML.de XML full text and ECLI for case law. It is federal only and its dataset is incomplete ([DigitalService](https://digitalservice.bund.de/en/blog/neuris-legal-information-service-published-in-the-test-phase)). Land law sits in 16 separate portals, such as gesetze-bayern.de and recht.nrw.de, with no common API.

**Advice regulation.** RDG § 5 permits legal services only as an ancillary part of the occupation. The BGH has drawn the line narrowly (cases in section 1). The architects' chamber of NRW publishes guidance accordingly ([AKNW PH10, Dec 2023](https://www.aknw.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Praxishinweise/PH10_Rechtsdienstleistungsgesetz_Stand-Dezember-2023.pdf)).

**Calendars.** Public holidays are set by each Land. Some are municipal: the Augsburger Friedensfest (8 August) is a holiday only in the city of Augsburg. The holiday calendar therefore attaches to the territorial graph down to municipal level.

### 2.3 Switzerland

**Jurisdiction.** The Confederation sets the frame for spatial planning (RPG/RPV), environmental protection, disability equality (BehiG), energy targets and the property-restriction cadastre. The 26 cantons legislate building and planning law (Planungs- und Baugesetz PBG, Baugesetz BauG and similar), fire protection (through an intercantonal concordat), energy-in-buildings and cantonal monument protection. Municipalities issue the Bau- und Zonenordnung (BZO) or Baureglement and the Zonenplan, which the canton approves. The building authority is usually the municipality, with cantonal coordination for special permissions such as building outside the building zone (Art. 24 RPG). Three territorial levels plus intercantonal concordats, a fourth "virtual" layer that is neither federal nor cantonal.

**Concordats and model regulations.** Three are relevant, each with its own adoption pattern.
- **IVHB**, the intercantonal agreement harmonising building terms and measurement methods. Cantons *accede*, or, like Zurich, implement the terms without acceding ([Kanton Zürich](https://www.zh.ch/de/planen-bauen/baubewilligung/infos-baubehoerden-fachstellen/harmonisierung-baubegriffe.html)). I found 14 member cantons as of 2014 and no authoritative current list. Even the *definition* of "Gebäudehöhe" or "Geschossflächenziffer" depends on whether, and when, the canton and then the municipality converted.
- **VKF fire protection regulations (BSV 2015)**, declared binding by the IOTH (Interkantonales Organ Technische Handelshemmnisse) for all cantons. The total revision "BSV 2026" was paused in January 2026 after the Crans-Montana fire and now targets entry into force around autumn 2027 ([organisator.ch, Feb 2026](https://www.organisator.ch/en/?p=44178); [BFH project](https://www.bfh.ch/de/forschung/forschungsprojekte/2019-807-294-441/)). The edition name ("2026") and its effective date have separated, so an edition label is not a date.
- **MuKEn**, the cantons' model energy regulations. The EnDK adopted MuKEn 2025 on 29 August 2025. Cantons transpose them into cantonal energy law over typically one to three years, and MuKEn 2014 reached St. Gallen only in autumn 2020 ([EnDK MuKEn 2025](https://endk.ch/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/MuKEn2025_f-2025-08-29.pdf); [Kanton St. Gallen](https://www.ratsinfo.sg.ch/media/documents/published/031d82e6-9154-4b3a-bc6e-0009b499d8b9.pdf)). That is a six-year lag on the same model.

**Standards.** SIA norms (SIA 416 areas and volumes, SIA 500 barrier-free building, SIA 380/1 thermal energy, SIA 118 general conditions for construction works, SIA 102 architects' services and fees, SIA 112 phase model) are private and paid. They become binding by cantonal reference or by contract. SIA 102 and SIA 112 define the phase model (strategic planning, preliminary studies, design, tendering, realisation, operation) that Swiss offices think in. It differs from HOAI's nine LPH.

**Land-use data.** The **ÖREB-Kataster** (cadastre of public-law property restrictions) is a federal obligation run by the cantons. It covers all 26 cantons since 2021, with land-use planning as its "Herzstück", and offers dynamic and static extracts, a web service and a WFS on opendata.swiss ([law.ch, Nov 2021](https://law.ch/2021/11/zugang-zum-oereb-kataster-nun-in-allen-kantonen); [opendata.swiss WFS](https://opendata.swiss/it/dataset/wfs-oreb-kataster-offentlich-rechtliche-eigentumsbeschrankungen)). Per parcel it lists the restrictions *with links to the legal provisions* (ÖREBlex). This is the clearest example of a public connector that returns parcel → applicable legal provisions.

**Legal data.** Fedlex runs a SPARQL endpoint (`fedlex.data.admin.ch/sparqlendpoint`) using the JOLux ontology, built on FRBR and ELI, with URIs such as `eli/cc/2017/762/20221001` for a consolidation. New publications have been Akoma-Ntoso-compatible XML since 30 May 2022 ([Swiss Federal Chancellery slides, June 2024](https://bfh.ch/dam/jcr:1bd193e1-805d-4f77-baf7-47fa71ffb25f/02_Linked-Data-Meetup_Jun24_BK.pdf); [JOLux docs](https://swiss.github.io/fedlex-jolux/introduction.html)). Cantonal law sits in 26 systematic collections, aggregated by LexFind.ch, and many run on common platforms (the `*.clex.ch` and `lex.*.ch` frontends).

**Languages.** Four national languages. Federal acts are published in German, French and Italian, and the three texts are equally authentic. Romansh is used for acts of particular importance. Cantons choose their official languages. Bern, Fribourg and Valais are bilingual (de/fr) and Graubünden trilingual (de/it/rm). A Fribourg municipality may publish its Baureglement only in French. Terminology also differs from Germany and Austria: "Bauherrschaft", "Baugesuch", "Baubewilligung", "Geschossfläche" defined through IVHB, "Grenzabstand".

**Advice and deadlines.** Switzerland has no general federal monopoly on out-of-court legal advice. The federal Lawyers Act (BGFA) regulates court representation, and cantonal rules vary, so I could not confirm this per canton. Procedural deadlines include court holidays (Fristenstillstand), e.g. under Art. 46 BGG at Easter, from 15 July to 15 August, and from 18 December to 2 January. Cantonal administrative procedure has its own variants. Public holidays are cantonal, with 1 August the only federal one.

### 2.4 Liechtenstein

A single-level case. Baugesetz of 11 December 2008 (LGBl. 2009 Nr. 44), one national building authority (Amt für Hochbau und Raumplanung, called Amt für Bau und Infrastruktur until March 2022), and two procedure types, permit and notification. 924 permits were granted in 2023 ([Statistikportal.li](https://www.statistikportal.li/de/publikation/bautaetigkeit-2023/2023/05/v-1/p26983/pdf); [AHR Verfahrensablauf](https://www.llv.li/serviceportal2/amtsstellen/amt-fuer-hochbau-und-raumplanung/verfahrensablauf-bauordnung.pdf)). Municipalities keep zoning plans and building regulations. Liechtenstein is in the EEA, not the EU, and in a customs union with Switzerland. In practice it draws on Swiss technical rules (SIA, VKF), but I could not verify the binding mechanism online. Law is published at gesetze.li. For the model, the competent authority sits at *country* level while zoning stays municipal. The authority level is data, not a fixed tier.

### 2.5 Italy / South Tyrol

Italy has national building law (DPR 380/2001, Testo unico dell'edilizia), national technical standards (NTC 2018 for structures) and UNI standards. The Autonomous Province of Bolzano/South Tyrol has primary legislative competence for spatial planning. Landesgesetz Nr. 9/2018 "Raum und Landschaft" entered into force in July 2020 and has been amended several times (LG 17/2019, 15/2020, 9/2023). It requires a municipal development programme and a Gemeindeplan für Raum und Landschaft (Art. 51–54). A province-wide *model building regulation* (Musterbauordnung) unifies the 116 municipal Bauordnungen ([stol.it](https://www.stol.it/artikel/politik/neue-musterbauordnung-schafft-einheit-unter-116-suedtiroler-gemeinden); [LexBrowser](https://lexbrowser.provinz.bz.it/doc/de/LG%C2%A7XXX%C2%A7L/landesgesetzgebung/raumordnung/l.aspx)). So the model-and-adoption pattern recurs *inside* one province. Brixen's 2025 building regulation is a municipal adoption ([brixen.it](https://www.brixen.it/verordnungen/bauordnung_8704/genehmigte%20bauordnung%202025.pdf)).

Languages: German and Italian are co-official, and Ladin in the Ladin valleys. Under Art. 99 of the Autonomy Statute, German is equal to Italian, but the **Italian wording prevails for acts of a legislative nature**. This is from my reading of DPR 670/1972; the provincial PDF was not retrievable on 2026-11-04, so verify the text. An architect in Bruneck reads the German text, and the authoritative text is the Italian one. LexBrowser carries a time-travel view ("In vigore al", in force on date X), which is a point-in-time access pattern.

South Tyrol is also the cleanest test that "country" is the wrong unit for some decisions. Planning law is provincial, building-product and structural law is national, and the professional chamber (Architektenkammer Bozen) is provincial within the national Ordine.

## 3. Breadth: five other systems

### 3.1 France
Unitary state with strong central codes and decentralised urban planning. **Code de la construction et de l'habitation (CCH)**: the regulatory part of Book I was recodified by Décret 2021-872 of 30 June 2021, in force 1 July 2021, with concordance tables between old and new article numbers ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000043890808)). An article number therefore changes identity across a recodification, and the model needs a "renumbered-from" relation. **Code de l'urbanisme**: PLU / PLUi as municipal or intercommunal zoning. Since 1 January 2023 a PLU is **enforceable only once published on the Géoportail de l'urbanisme**, digitised to the CNIG standard (version 2024-01 validated 14 Dec 2023) ([collectivites-locales.gouv.fr](https://www.collectivites-locales.gouv.fr/index.php/FAQ/la-publicite-des-documents-durbanisme); [CNIG](https://cnig.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/20240702-_dossier_validationstandards.pdf)). Classification: ERP (établissements recevant du public) by *type* (use) and *catégorie* (1–5 by occupancy), dwellings by *famille* 1–4, IGH for high-rise. Standards: NF DTU documents are AFNOR standards that bind through contract and insurance (décennale), not through law. Energy: RE2020. Architects are compulsory above a floor-area threshold. Legal data: Légifrance API through PISTE (OAuth, beta), Etalab 2.0 licence, and Légifrance publishes ELI. Language: French only.

### 3.2 United Kingdom
Three building-control systems in one state. **England**: Building Act 1984 and Building Regulations 2010 state functional requirements. The **Approved Documents** are statutory guidance whose observance *tends to show* compliance (Building Act 1984 s. 7). That is a presumption-of-conformity mode, not a binding one. The Building Safety Act 2022 created the **Building Safety Regulator** as building control authority for higher-risk buildings: during design and construction, 18 m+ or 7+ storeys with at least two residential units, or a hospital or care home meeting the height criteria, with statutory exclusions (the occupation regime has its own criteria). Gateway 2 is a hard stop before works and Gateway 3 before occupation, except for work in an existing hospital or care home, where the occupation offence does not apply and the part may be used while the regulator assesses the completion certificate application ([BSR FAQ](https://buildingsafety.campaign.gov.uk/building-safety-regulator-making-buildings-safer/the-building-control-authority/building-control-faq-explainer/); [gov.uk criteria](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/criteria-for-determining-whether-a-new-building-that-is-being-designed-and-constructed-is-a-higher-risk-building)). Competence switches by *building characteristic*, not territory. **Scotland**: Building (Scotland) Act 2003 with domestic and non-domestic **Technical Handbooks**. New handbooks took effect on 6 April 2026, and *which edition applies depends on the date the building warrant application is submitted* or works start ([gov.scot](https://www.gov.scot/publications/building-standards-technical-handbook-domestic-april-2026-pdf/)). Verifiers are the local authorities. **Planning** (England): Town and Country Planning Act 1990, the NPPF, local plans and permitted development. Wales and Northern Ireland are separate again. Phases: RIBA Plan of Work stages 0–7. Contracts: JCT, NEC. Site safety: CDM 2015. Professional title: ARB under the Architects Act 1997 protects the title, not the function. Legal data: legislation.gov.uk with CLML native XML, AKN output, point-in-time URLs (`/ukpga/2020/17/2022-07-21/`), Atom feeds and a publication log, free under OGL ([data documentation](https://legislation.github.io/data-documentation/api/overview.html)). Approved Documents and Technical Handbooks are gov.uk / gov.scot publications outside that system. Language: English, and English and Welsh equal in Wales.

### 3.3 United States
The extreme case of model and adoption. The ICC publishes the I-codes (IBC, IRC, IECC, IFC, IEBC) on a three-year cycle. States adopt by statute or rule, often with amendments, and many states leave adoption to cities and counties. North Dakota's state code moves to the new edition on 1 January 2026. Clark County, NV published its own 2024 IBC amendments, revised 22 July 2026, which become law only when a jurisdiction adopts them ([ND Commerce](https://www.commerce.nd.gov/building-codes); [Clark County](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:2384b32c-905a-4141-92f9-2c906d1ccd1c/original/as/2024-IBC-Amendments---CLARK-COUNTY-AMENDED%28R2%29-07-22-2026.pdf)). The ICC says amendment detail must be obtained from the jurisdiction itself ([ICC adoption chart](https://www.concrete.org/Portals/0/Files/PDF/MasterICodeAdoptionChart.pdf)). NFPA codes (NFPA 101, NFPA 70) are adopted the same way. Zoning is entirely local, with municipal codes hosted by commercial publishers. Federal overlays include the ADA Standards and the Fair Housing Act. Classification: occupancy groups (A, B, E, F, H, I, M, R, S, U) × construction types I–V (A/B). Licensing: architects are licensed per state, and unauthorised practice of law is defined per state. Copyright: *ASTM v. Public.Resource.Org*, D.C. Cir., 12 Sept 2023 (82 F.4th 1262), held non-commercial posting of incorporated standards to be fair use and left open whether copyright survives incorporation ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/cadc/22-7063/22-7063-2023-09-12.html)). The **Pro Codes Act** (H.R. 4072, introduced 23 June 2025; Senate companion S. 4145) would keep copyright for standards bodies that post incorporated standards free and read-only. An October 2026 coalition letter asks for a Judiciary Committee vote, so it is not enacted as of this date ([ASHRAE-hosted letter](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/about/government%20affairs/public%20policy%20resources/hjc-pro-codes-act-coalition-october-2026-final.pdf); [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr4072ih/html/BILLS-119hr4072ih.htm)).

### 3.4 Sweden
Plan- och bygglagen (PBL 2010:900) and its ordinance (PBF). Boverket's regulations are binding "föreskrifter" with non-binding "allmänna råd" (general advice) printed beside them, two bindingness levels in one document. The old BBR and EKS were replaced by a topic-structured series, **BFS 2024:6–13**, in force **1 July 2025**. Until 30 June 2026 the developer could **choose** the old or new rules, but had to apply the chosen set *in its entirety*. Energy stays in BBR until BFS 2024:14 takes effect on **1 October 2026** ([Boverket](https://www.boverket.se/en/start/laws-and-regulations/national-regulations/building-regulations/)). Land use: municipal detaljplan and översiktsplan, held as digital plans under Lantmäteriet's national rules. Standards: SS-EN through SIS (paid). Roles: "kontrollansvarig" (independent inspector) is a statutory role in the procedure.

### 3.5 Netherlands
The **Omgevingswet** entered into force on 1 January 2024. It merged dozens of acts into one act with four orders in council: Bal (activities), **Bbl** (buildings: building, use, demolition), Bkl (quality, including what the omgevingsplan must contain) and Ob ([IPLO](https://iplo.nl/regelgeving/omgevingswet/inhoud/omgevingswet)). Each municipality has **one omgevingsplan**. At commencement it was seeded with national rules (the "bruidsschat") that municipalities then rewrite. Plans are published as machine-readable rules linked to locations through the national **DSO** (Digitaal Stelsel Omgevingswet) and the Omgevingsloket. APIs are open, with keys from the Ontwikkelaarsportaal ([IPLO for developers](https://iplo.nl/regelgeving/omgevingswet/introductie/uitleg-bedrijfsleven/informatie-softwareleveranciers-ontwikkelaars/)). The Wkb (quality assurance in building) moved technical checking for consequence class 1 to private quality assurers from the same date. NEN standards (NEN 2580 areas) are referenced by the Bbl and are paid. The Netherlands is the strongest existing example of "rules as data, linked to geometry", and its own standards (STOP/TPOD) are a reference design for how a land-use rule can carry location, activity and norm values.

## 4. Cross-cutting patterns the data model must express

### 4.1 Jurisdiction: territory graph plus competence graph
- **Territory** is a hierarchy with variable depth. Austria has country → Land → (Bezirk) → Gemeinde. Germany has country → Land → Regierungsbezirk → Kreis → Gemeinde. Switzerland has country → Kanton → Gemeinde (with Bezirke in some cantons). Italy has country → Region → autonomous province → Comune. Liechtenstein has country → Gemeinde. The US has country → state → county → city or township. Plus the EU above, and intercantonal concordats or model-code bodies *beside* the hierarchy. A fixed three-level "Land, Bundesland, Gemeinde" (ticket 31's wording) works for Austria but fails for Germany's county-level authorities, US counties and South Tyrol.
- **Competence** is a separate relation: (authority, function, territory, condition). Examples: German untere Bauaufsicht = Kreis for most municipalities, the city itself for large ones. England: BSR for higher-risk buildings, otherwise the local authority or a registered building control approver. Austria: mayor for building law, Bundesdenkmalamt for heritage, Bezirkshauptmannschaft for commercial-installation law, the Land for nature protection. The NL Wkb moves technical checks to a private quality assurer by consequence class. The *condition* is often a project fact such as height, use or a listed-building flag. Ticket 31's "Behörde" is the result of evaluating this relation, not a lookup from the address alone.
- **Implication for ticket 31.** Verified address → coordinates and parcel → territorial chain (as deep as the country has) → candidate authorities per *function* (building permit, heritage, fire, nature) → final authority once project facts are known. Ticket 32's rule that a Weisung applies only "für die Baubehörde meines Projekts" needs exactly this resolved authority.

### 4.2 Instrument types and the bindingness relation
Every system examined uses a ladder like this, with local names:

| Layer | AT | DE | CH | UK (E) | US | SE | NL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Statute | BO, ROG (Land) | BauGB, LBO | RPG, PBG | Building Act 1984 | state statutes | PBL | Omgevingswet |
| Ordinance | BTV / OIB adoption | BauNVO, VV TB | BauV, BZO | Building Regs 2010 | adopted code | PBF, BFS föreskrift | Bbl, omgevingsplan |
| Model / intergovernmental | OIB-RL | MBO, MVV TB | IVHB, BSV, MuKEn | – | ICC, NFPA | – | – |
| Statutory guidance | – | – | VKF Brandschutzrichtlinien | Approved Documents | – | allmänna råd | – |
| Standards | ÖNORM | DIN, EN | SIA, EN | BS, EN | ASTM, ASHRAE | SS-EN | NEN |
| Interpretation / practice | Erlässe, Weisungen, commentary | Einführungserlasse, commentary | Merkblätter, case law | circulars | code commentary | Boverket guidance | – |

What changes is not the ladder but the **binding mode** linking a higher instrument to a lower one:
- *Declared binding* (Verbindlicherklärung): a Land ordinance declares OIB-RL 2 Ausgabe 2023 binding, or NormG § 9 for ÖNORMs.
- *Incorporation by reference*: static (a dated edition) or dynamic (the current edition). MVV TB lists DIN editions statically. US adoption ordinances name the edition year.
- *Presumption of conformity*: Approved Documents (s. 7 Building Act 1984), harmonised EN standards under EU product law, MVV TB with deviation allowed on proof of equivalence.
- *Recognised rules of technology* (a.a.R.d.T., Stand der Technik): binding through civil liability, with standards as rebuttable evidence.
- *Contract incorporation*: VOB/B, ÖNORM B 2110, SIA 118, NF DTU, JCT.
- *Recommendation*: allmänna råd, Merkblätter, commentary.

Plus the *derogation* relations: "deviates from" (a Land adopting OIB with changes), "supersedes" (GModG supersedes GEG; BFS 2024:6 supersedes BBR section 5), "renumbered from" (CCH 2021) and "expires" (§ 246e on 31 Dec 2030).

Ticket 30 asks for exactly this ("verbindlich macht, verdrängt oder davon abweicht"). Its own note warns that ADR-0025 deliberately cut back an earlier design with typed relations. The comparative evidence says the cut cannot go below a small closed set of relation types (makes-binding with a mode, deviates-from, supersedes, renumbered-from, interprets), because without them the Austrian table above cannot be expressed.

**Interpretation is not a source of a requirement, binding precedent can be.** In common-law jurisdictions a binding holding can itself establish a requirement, including a duty no statute states, so it is a requirement-bearing item; commentary and non-binding interpretation are not. Ticket 32 says commentaries are read along "aber nie als Quelle einer Anforderung" and that a Weisung counts as authority practice only for its authority. The comparative picture holds: non-binding case law, commentary, ministerial circulars and authority directives are an *interprets* relation onto a requirement-bearing item, scoped to an authority or jurisdiction. They are the same item primitive with a different role.

### 4.3 Model codes and adoption
Seen in AT (OIB), DE (MBO→LBO, MVV TB→VV TB), CH (IVHB, BSV, MuKEn), South Tyrol (Musterbauordnung→116 municipal Bauordnungen) and US (I-codes→states→cities). Properties of an adoption, all observed:
- the adopting jurisdiction and instrument (e.g. OÖ BauTV-Novelle 2025),
- the model edition (OIB-RL 2023), the *scope* (RL 1–5 only), the effective date,
- deviations, as text or as replaced clauses,
- *skipping* (Tirol: RL 6 2019 straight to 2025),
- *lag*, sometimes years (MuKEn 2014 reaching St. Gallen in 2020),
- *non-accession with voluntary alignment* (Zurich and IVHB).

The model edition itself is an item with editions. Adoption is a dated relation from a jurisdiction's instrument to the model edition. If adoption were a field on the model, it could not express the Austrian table.

### 4.4 Time: in force, applicable, verified
Observed rules for "which edition applies to this project":

| Jurisdiction | Trigger for the applicable edition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Scotland | date the building warrant application is submitted, or start of works if no warrant | gov.scot 2026 |
| Saxony (Eurocode introduction) | start of works for exempted / procedure-free projects; date of grant for permit procedures | SMR Sachsen |
| Lower Austria (BO 2014 § 70) | pending proceedings finish under the old law (filing before entry into force) | NÖ BO § 70 |
| Vienna 2023 (demolition) | filing after a cut-off date *before* entry into force | MA 37 letter |
| NRW (LBO transitions) | applicant's **choice** in defined cases | AKNW Dienstbesprechung 2017 |
| Sweden 2025 | applicant's choice until 30 Jun 2026, all-or-nothing per rule set | Boverket |
| Germany, BauNVO | the edition in force when the *Bebauungsplan* was adopted | BauNVO transitional rules |
| Germany, § 246e BauGB | available only until 31 Dec 2030 | GÖRG |
| US local adoptions | effective date of the adopting ordinance; permit application date per local rule | local codes |

Consequences:
1. The rule is **per jurisdiction × instrument (sometimes per provision) × procedure type**. It is not a single per-country setting.
2. It can carry **options** (an applicant's choice), which ticket 31 must show as a decision to confirm, not resolve silently. The ticket already demands "zeigt es mir, statt es stillschweigend zu wählen".
3. The anchor can be a **project event** (filing, grant, start of works), a **plan event** (adoption of the Bebauungsplan) or a **calendar sunset**.
4. **Edition labels are not dates.** "BSV 2026" will take effect around 2027. "OIB-RL 6 Ausgabe 2025" took effect in Vienna in July 2026 and nowhere else yet.

Ticket 31 captures submission date, permit date and project start, or the period for completed projects. Those are exactly the events the rules above key on. Ticket 30's note that the Stichtag rule "hängt an den Übergangsbestimmungen und ist noch offen" is confirmed. It can only be closed as **data per jurisdiction**, authored the same way as the rest.

A third axis comes from ticket 32: **knowledge time**. When was this edition last verified, and is a newer one known? Ticket 32 asks for a freshness marker shared between platform and office items ("eine Messgröße, zwei Orte"). Bitemporal modelling, separating valid time from recorded/verified time, is the standard answer.

### 4.5 Classification schemes
- AT (OIB): GK 1–5 by escape level, storeys, use units, floor area. Plus special-building categories (Beherbergungsstätten, Verkaufsstätten, Garagen) with their own thresholds.
- DE (MBO § 2): GK 1–5 by height (floor level of the highest storey with a habitable room) and use-unit size, plus "Sonderbauten" by list.
- CH (VKF): buildings of low height (≤11 m total height), medium height (≤30 m) and high-rise (>30 m), plus use categories.
- UK (England): purpose groups (Approved Document B) plus the BSA "higher-risk building" status.
- US: IBC occupancy groups × construction types.
- FR: ERP type × category, dwelling famille, IGH.
- SE: building classes (Br0–Br3) and activity classes (Vk1–Vk6) for fire.

Same-named classes ("GK 4" in AT versus DE) are **not** interchangeable, and the inputs (Fluchtniveau versus height of the top floor) use different measurement definitions. Ticket 31's "Einstufung Bestand vs. nach Umsetzung" means each scheme needs (inputs, derivation rule, edition, jurisdiction) so it can be evaluated twice, on existing and planned facts, with the delta shown. Ticket 30 already requires derived values to be "mit ihrer Grundlage zur Bestätigung" presented.

### 4.6 Measurement rules
ÖNORM B 1800 (AT), DIN 277 (DE; DIN 276 for costs), SIA 416 (CH; IVHB for planning measures), NEN 2580 (NL), "surface de plancher" (FR, Code de l'urbanisme), RICS/IPMS (UK). Planning-law measures differ again from building-law measures: German GFZ under BauNVO, Swiss Geschossflächenziffer under IVHB as transposed by the canton, Austrian Bebauungsdichte under each Land's planning law. A derived value such as floor area must name its measurement rule and edition. Otherwise a Swiss gross floor area could feed an Austrian GK derivation.

### 4.7 Procedures, authorities, deadlines, calendars
Each jurisdiction defines procedure types (full permit, simplified, notification, exemption, procedure-free) with thresholds that are themselves classification-dependent. The procedure type matters three times: for which authority is competent, for which Stichtag rule applies (Saxony) and for which deadlines run. Deadlines are statutory durations with computation rules (AT §§ 32–33 AVG; DE § 31 VwVfG with § 193 BGB; CH court holidays) and fictions (DE § 36 BauGB two-month consent fiction; § 36a BauGB; Genehmigungsfiktion in several LBOs). Holidays attach to the territory graph at Land, canton and even municipal level (Augsburg). A deadline is (trigger event, duration, computation rule, calendar of the competent authority's territory).

### 4.8 Land-use plans as spatial data
Four maturity levels exist side by side:
1. **The GIS dataset is the legal instrument**: France (PLU enforceable on GPU publication, from 2023) and the Netherlands (omgevingsplan rules linked to locations in DSO).
2. **An official cadastre maps the parcel to legal restrictions**: Switzerland's ÖREB-Kataster (in all cantons since 2021, with links to the provisions).
3. **A binding exchange standard with uneven coverage**: Germany's XPlanung (binding since 1 Feb 2023, many legacy PDFs).
4. **Viewer or PDF only**: much of Austria's municipal Flächenwidmung and Bebauungspläne, and US municipal zoning codes as text plus maps.

For ticket 31 ("the agent sucht gezielt nach öffentlich verfügbaren Informationen zum Standort … legt Funde mit Quelle zur Bestätigung vor"), the connector's answer has three possible statuses. At level 1 it is *law*. At level 2 it is an *official extract*. At levels 3–4 it is a *document to read*, and when it is unreadable, a request to the person for the specific plan (ticket 31: "fordert the agent genau dieses Dokument an"). Ticket 30's per-region list of decisive facts ("Bebauungsplan, Stellplatzregeln, Denkmalschutz oder Gefahrenzonen") is the place to record, per region, which level the source sits at and where it lives. The same holds for hazard zones (AT federal/Land plans, CH cantonal hazard maps), heritage registers (AT BDA list, German Land lists, CH ISOS/cantonal inventories), and snow, wind and seismic zones, which are usually tables or maps in a standard or national annex, so a licensed source.

### 4.9 Licensing of standards and what it means for ingestion
- Statutes and ordinances are public and openly licensed: RIS CC BY 4.0, Légifrance Etalab 2.0, legislation.gov.uk OGL, Fedlex and NeuRIS open.
- Model regulations from intergovernmental bodies are usually free (OIB PDFs, MBO, MVV TB from DIBt, VKF BSV, MuKEn). The ICC is the exception: model codes are paid, with read-only free access for some.
- National standards are paid and copyrighted (Austrian Standards, DIN Media, SIA, NEN, SIS, AFNOR, BSI), even when binding. Countervailing law: AT NormG § 9; CJEU C-588/21 P (5 Mar 2024, ECLI:EU:C:2024:201) on access to harmonised standards as part of EU law ([Verfassungsblog, 19 Mar 2024](https://verfassungsblog.de/eu-harmonised-standards/)); US *ASTM v. PRO* (2023) and the pending Pro Codes Act.

Implications: the platform can hold **metadata and references** for every standard (designation, edition, who makes it binding, how), but generally **not the text**. Text arrives through (a) an office's own licence (ticket 32), isolated to that office, and only where that licence also permits hosted AI processing, indexing and quotation (office-wide access alone does not), or (b) a future licensed feed (ticket 32 names "direkt über Austrian Standards" as the eventual route). The licensing status per edition is data (open, licensed-platform, licensed-office-only, reference-only), and it drives both ingestion and what the agent may quote. feld72 asked for zero retention specifically for standards (ticket 32 note), so retention policy must be expressible per item class or licence, not only per organisation.

### 4.10 Identifiers and formats
- **ELI** (European Legislation Identifier): RIS (AT), Fedlex (CH), NeuRIS (DE federal), Légifrance (FR), legislation.gov.uk, EUR-Lex. ELI URIs encode work, point in time, version and language (e.g. `.../2025-01-01/1/deu`), which matches the edition × language need.
- **Akoma Ntoso / LegalDocML** (OASIS): Fedlex since 2022, legislation.gov.uk AKN output, LegalDocML.de for German federal drafting and NeuRIS output. It gives article-level structure for citation anchors.
- **ECLI** for case law (NeuRIS, EU, many national courts).
- **Standards**: no shared identifier scheme. Use "body + number + part + edition date" (e.g. "ÖNORM B 1800:2013-08-01", "DIN 277:2021-08", "SIA 416:2003"). Model regulations carry edition labels ("OIB-RL 2, Ausgabe Mai 2023"; "MVV TB 2025/1") that are not dates.
- **Spatial**: XPlanung (DE), CNIG PLU standard (FR), STOP/TPOD (NL), ÖREB data model (CH), INSPIRE themes across the EU.

The data model should keep its own stable item identifier and map external identifiers (ELI, ECLI, standard designation, GPU id) as aliases, because no single scheme covers standards, model codes, directives and office items.

### 4.11 Languages
- Authoritative language(s) per instrument: CH federal de/fr/it equally authentic; South Tyrol de/it with Italian prevailing for legislative acts; bilingual cantons; Wales.
- The answer language follows the user. The source language follows the instrument. A translation is never authoritative and must be marked as such.
- Cross-lingual retrieval is required in CH and South Tyrol, where a German question must find French or Italian provisions.
- Terminology variants inside German (AT/DE/CH) are domain data, not i18n strings: "Bauwerber" (AT) / "Bauherr" (DE) / "Bauherrschaft" (CH); "Einreichung" / "Bauantrag" / "Baugesuch"; "Bescheid" / "Baugenehmigung" / "Baubewilligung"; "Flächenwidmungsplan" / "Flächennutzungsplan + Bebauungsplan" / "Zonenplan + BZO".

### 4.12 Legal-advice regulation
- DE: RDG § 5 with a narrow BGH line (I ZR 227/19; VII ZR 190/22). Architects may apply building and planning law within the design task, but may not represent clients in objection proceedings or draft contract clauses.
- AT: the lawyers' reserve (RAO, ban on "Winkelschreiberei") applies. Ziviltechniker have statutory powers within their field, which I could not confirm online in detail.
- CH: no general extrajudicial monopoly at federal level; cantonal variation possible.
- UK: only "reserved legal activities" (Legal Services Act 2007) are restricted.
- US: unauthorised practice of law is defined per state.

Per jurisdiction, then: what the agent may *assert* (what a provision says, what applies to the stated facts, with the source) versus what it must *hedge* (an individual legal assessment, contract drafting, representation), plus the disclaimer text. This is per-country data, and it is connected to the professional law the platform already models (chamber, Bauvorlageberechtigung).

### 4.13 The areas in MARKET SCOPE all fit the same shapes
| Area | Typical instruments | Data shape | Varies at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building codes, technical rules | statute + ordinance + model adoption + standards | corpus + relations + editions | country / state |
| Zoning, local plans | municipal by-law, GIS | connector / spatial data + corpus | municipality |
| Permit procedure, authorities | procedural law, competence rules | reference data (competence, procedure types) | state / county / municipality |
| Fire, accessibility, energy | statute + model + standards (VKF, OIB-RL 2/4/6, GModG, MuKEn, SIA 500, B 1600) | corpus + editions + adoption | state / canton |
| Heritage | federal (AT) or state (DE, CH) law + registers | corpus + connector (register) | varies by country |
| EIA, environment | EU directive → national/state law | corpus + thresholds as reference data | country / state |
| Neighbour law, easements | civil code + land register | corpus + connector (land register) | country (some state law) |
| Procurement of planning services | EU directive, BVergG, GWB/VgV, BöB/IVöB | corpus + time-versioned thresholds | EU / country / state |
| Fees | HOAI, SIA 102, AT models | reference data (phases, tables), often office overlay | country / office |
| Professional law | ZTG, Architektengesetze, LBO § 65 | corpus + reference data (who may sign) | country / state |
| Construction contracts | ÖNORM B 2110, VOB/B, SIA 118, JCT, AIA | standard (licensed) + contract binding mode | country |
| Site safety | Directive 92/57 → BauKG, BaustellV, CDM 2015 | corpus + thresholds | country |
| Calendars, deadlines | holiday laws, procedural codes | reference data | state / canton / municipality |

None of these areas needs a new primitive. Each is an item with editions, linked by the same relations, scoped to a territory, sometimes reached through a connector, sometimes carrying reference values. That is the main evidence for the founder's "no new primitive per area".

## 5. What this implies for the model (requirements, not design)

The design stage should treat these as acceptance tests derived from the evidence:

1. **Territory nodes with variable depth and typed levels**, plus a **competence relation** (authority, function, territory, condition on project facts). Test: the Austrian heritage track, the German Kreis authority, the English BSR and the Liechtenstein national authority are all expressible without special cases.
2. **Item → edition**, where an edition has an in-force interval (possibly with a known end), a language set with the authoritative language(s) marked, a licence status, external identifiers, and a structural anchor scheme (article/paragraph via AKN where available). Test: OIB-RL 6 with three editions in force in different Länder on the same day.
3. **A closed set of typed relations between editions**: makes-binding (mode: declared, static reference, dynamic reference, presumption, recognised rules, contract), deviates-from, supersedes, renumbered-from, interprets (role: commentary, directive, case law, scoped to an authority). Test: MVV TB 2025/1 → Niedersachsen VV TB (Nov 2025) with deviations → DIN edition, static.
4. **Adoption as a dated relation with scope**, not a property of the model. Test: the OIB table in 2.1, cell by cell.
5. **Reference-date rules as authored data** (jurisdiction × instrument scope × procedure type → trigger event, options, sunset), evaluated against project events that ticket 31 captures. Test: Scotland, Saxony, Sweden (choice), BauNVO (plan date), § 246e (sunset).
6. **Classification schemes and measurement rules as data** (inputs, derivation, edition, jurisdiction), evaluable on existing and planned facts. Test: an extension by two storeys raising OIB GK 3 → GK 4 in Vienna, shown with its inputs.
7. **Decisive-fact catalogue per region** (fact, typical source document, connector if any, maturity level 1–4 of 4.8, what to request if missing). Ticket 30 already asks for this list. Test: Bebauungsplan in Bavaria (XPlanung or PDF), Zonenplan in Zurich (ÖREB), PLU in Lyon (GPU), Flächenwidmung in Graz (Land GIS).
8. **Connectors as declared capabilities per territory**, whose results carry source, retrieval time and legal status (law / official extract / document), and become *secured* only after the person confirms (ticket 31: "erst dann gilt es als gesichert").
9. **Owner and visibility on every item and relation** (platform versus organisation), so an office edition of ÖNORM B 1800 fills the platform's text-less reference *for that office only*, and an office Weisung applies only when the resolved authority matches (ticket 32). Test: two offices, one with the licence, one without, same project type, different answers, no leakage.
10. **Bitemporal freshness**: valid time (in force) apart from verified time (last checked), with one freshness measure shared by platform and office items (ticket 32).
11. **Advice policy, calendar and deadline rules as per-jurisdiction reference data**, versioned like everything else.

**Observations on the tickets themselves.** Ticket 30's notes still refer to the project-setup ticket as "29", and the setup ticket (31) says its design doc points to archived ticket 26. Ticket 32 notes refer to "Ticket 28" as the place for office norms, which may now be ticket 32 itself. Ticket 31's address criterion says "Land, Bundesland und Gemeinde", which is right for Austria and needs to read as "the territorial chain to the depth the country has" for Germany, Switzerland and South Tyrol. Ticket 30 asks for typed text-to-text relations while its note warns against returning to ADR-0025's typed relations. The evidence above supports a small, closed set (point 3) as the floor.
